Model comparison

Selection uses mean cross-validation average precision (AP). Test rows below compare each candidate at a common 0.5 cutoff, while the selected model’s review policy uses its validation cutoff.

AP summarises precision across recall levels; its no-skill reference is approximately the churn prevalence. ROC-AUC assesses ranking, not probability calibration.

Score reliability

Scores have not been recalibrated. They are estimates, not guaranteed probabilities for a new customer population.

Which inputs matter to prediction?

Each feature is shuffled while the fitted model stays fixed. A larger AP decrease means the model relies more on that feature. Error bars show variation across five shuffles, not confidence intervals.

These are global associations, not explanations of why an individual will leave. Correlated billing fields can share importance. Permutation can also create unrealistic feature combinations.

What can the business test next?

Use observed patterns to propose onboarding or service-review experiments. Randomise outreach within eligible groups and compare retention and cost outcomes against a control group.

Changing a contract, payment method, or add-on in this model is not evidence that changing it in real life prevents churn.

Before operational use, define the scoring date and churn horizon, verify feature availability, validate on later customers, review subgroup errors, and monitor drift.
